About Me

I am an MSc Photonics student at Friedrich Schiller University Jena, with a background in physics and research interests in nanophotonics, optical materials, semiconductor nanostructures, and optoelectronic devices.

I completed my BSc (Hons.) in Physics at Pandit Deendayal Energy University, India, where I graduated as a Gold Medalist. During my undergraduate studies, I developed a strong interest in experimental optics through research on the linear and nonlinear optical properties of ferrite-based thin films and laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS).

My current research interests are shaped primarily by my work at Friedrich Schiller University Jena, where I investigate hybrid semiconductor systems combining two-dimensional transition-metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) with one-dimensional nanowires. I am particularly interested in 1D–2D heterostructures, nanoscale field-effect devices, light–matter interaction in low-dimensional materials, and optoelectronic and nanophotonic device concepts.

I like to read suspence-novels and watch star-wars with my loved ones.

Research at Friedrich Schiller University Jena

At Friedrich Schiller University Jena, I work with the GUFOS group at the Institute of Solid State Physics (IFK) under Prof. Dr. Soavi, Giancarlo on hybrid nanoscale semiconductor devices based on transition-metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) and nanowires.

My work focuses on the development of hybrid field-effect transistors (FETs) that combine two-dimensional TMD materials with one-dimensional semiconductor nanowires. The project involves material synthesis, device fabrication, electrical and optical characterization, and cleanroom processing, providing hands-on experience with the fabrication and investigation of nanoscale optoelectronic devices.

Research at INNOVENT e.V.

I currently work as a Student Research Assistant at INNOVENT e.V. – Technologieentwicklung in Jena, within the Magnetics and Optics Department (MOS). My research focuses on the characterization of Pyrosil thin-film coatings on glass deposited using Catalytic Chemical Vapor Deposition (CCVD).

I use Brewster Angle Reflectometry together with data processing and analysis to investigate properties such as film thickness, refractive index, and surface characteristics. The work also involves studying how different deposition parameters influence the resulting optical properties of the coatings.

    Can Quantum-Mechanical Description of Physical Reality Be Considered Complete?
    A. Einstein*†, B. Podolsky*, and N. Rosen*
    Phys. Rev., New Jersey. More Information can be found here , May 1935
    Abs DOI HTML PDF Video

    In a complete theory there is an element corresponding to each element of reality. A sufficient condition for the reality of a physical quantity is the possibility of predicting it with certainty, without disturbing the system. In quantum mechanics in the case of two physical quantities described by non-commuting operators, the knowledge of one precludes the knowledge of the other. Then either (1) the description of reality given by the wave function in quantum mechanics is not complete or (2) these two quantities cannot have simultaneous reality. Consideration of the problem of making predictions concerning a system on the basis of measurements made on another system that had previously interacted with it leads to the result that if (1) is false then (2) is also false. One is thus led to conclude that the description of reality as given by a wave function is not complete.
